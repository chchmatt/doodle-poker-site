This policy is for the Doodle Poker app for Android and iOS, made by an independent developer (“I”, “me”).

The short version

Doodle Poker doesn’t collect, store or share any personal information. There are no ads, no analytics, no accounts and no tracking. Nothing you record is sent anywhere — unless you choose to use the optional AI review (see below), or share something yourself.

What the app keeps — on your device only

  • the poker hands and sessions you record;
  • your settings (such as blinds, currency, sound and vibration).

None of this is sent to me or to anyone else. It stays on your device until you uninstall the app or clear its data.

Optional: Claude review

The app can ask Claude (made by Anthropic) to look over a hand. This is off until you paste in your own Claude API key in Settings. Only when you press “Ask Claude API” on a hand (live, or as a batch, which Anthropic does in its own time and the app collects later), that hand is sent to Anthropic, using your key: its players (with the tags and notes you wrote about them), the actions, the cards that are known, the result and your notes. I never receive it. The key is kept in your device’s secure store, is left out of backups, and is sent only to Anthropic. What Anthropic does with the data is covered by Anthropic’s privacy policy. The answer is kept on the hand, on your device. A batch that is still waiting for its answer is kept on the hand too (its number, so the app can ask Anthropic for it), and is in your backups; the key never is.

You can also use any AI app without a key: the app copies the same text to your clipboard, and you paste it into the AI app of your choice. It leaves Doodle Poker only when you copy it or use the share button (which hands it to the app you pick), and what that app does with it is covered by that app’s own privacy policy. The answer you paste back is kept on the hand, on your device.

Backups and sharing

Backups, exports and “share as text” are made only when you ask. They are files or text handed to your device’s share sheet, and where they go is up to you. The app never sends them anywhere itself.

Permissions

The app uses the vibration (which you can turn off in Settings) and, if you turn them on, notifications that the device itself shows as break reminders (no server is involved). It doesn’t use your location, contacts, camera, microphone, photos or files.

The app stores

When you download the app from Google Play or the App Store, Google or Apple may collect information under their own privacy policies. I don’t receive any personal information from them.

Changes

If the app ever changes in a way that affects your privacy (for example, if a new feature sends data to a service), this policy will be updated first, and the date above changed.
