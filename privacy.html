Privacy statement

Version 1, 22 November 2026. This is always the newest version. The app asks you to agree to it the first time you open it, and again when its version goes up.

The short version

This statement is for the Doodle Poker app for Android and iOS, and for this website. “We” and “us” are the publisher of the app (below).

Doodle Poker keeps what you record on your phone, and, unless you say no, an encrypted copy in the cloud that we cannot read, so your records are not lost with the phone and can be used on another device. There are no ads, no analytics, no tracking and no accounts. Nothing is sold, and nothing is used for advertising. Things leave your phone only in the cases listed under “What leaves your phone”, each on its own line, and each happens because you asked for it (the encrypted copy because you chose it on the first screen or in Settings), except the exchange-rate lookup, which the app does by itself and which carries no personal information.

We have written this to meet the stricter privacy laws, so that it holds wherever the app is offered: the European Union’s and the United Kingdom’s (the GDPR), New Zealand’s Privacy Act 2020, Australia’s Privacy Act 1988, California’s laws (the CCPA and CPRA), Canada’s PIPEDA, and the duties most other countries share.

Who is responsible, and how to reach them

Doodle Poker is published by an individual, who is the person responsible for your information (the “controller” in European and British law). The publisher’s name and postal address will be written here before the app is released.

To ask about your information, use the Contact page or write to privacy@doodlepoker.com. We answer within a month. Because the app has no accounts, we cannot find what we keep about you from your name or email: to find it we need the reference that the app shows on its About page (under Privacy statement), which is made from a secret that only your phone holds.

What the app keeps on your phone only

  • the hands, sessions, expenses, trips, places, IOUs and people you record, with their notes and drawn faces;
  • photos you take or pick (of hands, receipts or Doodle Book pages) and the Doodle Book itself;
  • your settings, your goals and your practice results;
  • things you delete, kept in the Bin for 30 days;
  • if you add one, your Claude key, in your phone’s secure store;
  • a record of which version of this statement you agreed to, and when (see “Your agreement”).

None of this is sent to us or to anyone else, except in the cases below (and the encrypted copy of your records, if you keep one: see 9). It stays on your phone until you delete it, uninstall the app or clear its data. The phone’s own security, and the app lock if you switch it on, protect it.

Notes about other people

You keep notes, pictures and drawn faces of real people (the players, dealers and others you meet at the tables). They are your own records, kept on your own phone, and you are responsible for what you write and for using it fairly. The app does not send them anywhere unless you publish a hand that includes a name (you choose the names, and each can be replaced by “Villain 1” and so on), or send a hand to Claude with your own key (below). Please do not put in anything you would not want read out to the person it is about.

What leaves your phone

Each line says what is sent, when, why, where it goes, for how long it is kept, and the legal ground. “Ground: you asked” means it is needed to do what you asked of the app, and “Ground: your agreement” means you agreed to it by choosing it; you can stop at any time.

1. A published hand

  • What: the hand as you chose to publish it (you choose the names, which notes, tags, pictures and AI reviews go with it), and the picture of its table when there is one. With it go a secret key your phone made (see 2) and the time to keep it.
  • When: only when you choose Share, then doodlepoker.com, on a hand.
  • Why: to give the hand a page that anyone with its link can read.
  • Where: a small service we run, stored on Microsoft Azure servers in the United States. Its address is long and random, and the page is marked so that search engines leave it alone.
  • How long: until you take it down in the app (Settings, Hero’s published hands and live pages), or until the end of the time you chose; a daily sweep deletes it then. If you or anyone reports it through the Contact page we may take it down.
  • Ground: your agreement (you chose to publish it).

2. The key that says a hand or page is yours, and the counts that stop abuse

  • What: a secret key the app makes once and keeps in your phone’s secure store. It is sent with each publish and take-down. We keep only a one-way hash of it next to what you published. It is not in a backup and is not linked to your name, email or any account. (If you add a device with its code, this key goes to that device, through a short-lived encrypted envelope: see 9. If you keep an encrypted copy, the key that owns what you have published is also kept in it, encrypted so that only your own devices can open it, so that any of your devices can take down what you published: a linked browser is never given it.) We also count how many hands, pages and updates a key and an internet address make in a day; the address is kept only as a hash and only for a day or two.
  • Why: so only you can take your page down, and to stop abuse.
  • How long: the encrypted key goes with what it belongs to; the counts are deleted after about two days. Microsoft, our host, may keep ordinary server logs under its own rules.
  • Ground: you asked (it is how a page is yours), and our interest in keeping the service safe (the counts).

3. A live page of your stack

  • What: the stack of a session as it changes: the line of the chart (the shape only, which is a plain 0 to 100 line with no amounts, no stakes and no place, which is how it begins; or the stack in big blinds; or in money, or for a tournament in chips), the game as words, when it began, whether it is going, on a break or over, the place only if you choose to show it, a name and a line of words if you give them, and for a tournament the buy-in and rebuys, the blind level, the players left and a short note if you give them. No people, no notes of the session and no tags. It is sent with the same secret key as in 2.
  • When: only when you turn it on for a session (its Log tab, Go live). Each change then goes to the page by itself, unless you tick Not yet. A live page that says where you are and how much is in front of you is a risk to you, and the app says so beside the choice.
  • Why: so backers and friends can follow a session.
  • Where: Microsoft Azure, United States, as one small file replaced at each update. Anyone who has its link can read it.
  • How long: until the session ends and the days you chose have passed (from one day to 30, seven to begin with), or until you stop it or take it down; a page that is going but has heard nothing from your phone for three days is deleted.
  • Ground: your agreement (you turned it on).

4. Your agreement, kept in the cloud once you send something there

  • What: the version of this statement you agreed to, the date and time (our clock), the app’s version and what you chose, kept with the encrypted copy of your key from 2 (your “reference”). It is added to when you agree to a new version.
  • When: only once you have chosen something that sends data to our service (publishing a hand, turning on a live page, keeping an encrypted copy of your records in the cloud). Turning the copy off, and deleting it, are added to the record. A person who sends nothing has nothing of this kept anywhere but on their phone.
  • Why: so it can be shown later that you agreed.
  • Where: Microsoft Azure, United States.
  • How long: until six years after the last entry, or until you ask us to delete it, whichever is first.
  • Ground: our duty and interest in being able to show your agreement.

5. Claude, with your own key

The app can use Claude, made by Anthropic. It is off until you paste in your own Claude API key in Settings; nothing is sent to Anthropic before that. After that, each time you tap a button that uses Claude, what that feature needs goes from your phone straight to Anthropic (api.anthropic.com), using your key and under your own account with Anthropic. We never receive it. What Anthropic does with it is covered by Anthropic’s privacy policy, and Anthropic’s servers are outside your country.

  • Hand review (live, or as a batch that Anthropic answers in its own time and the app collects later): the blinds, ante, number of players and currency sign; each player’s position, the name you typed, the tags and notes you wrote about them, their starting stack and known hole cards; links between players; every action; the board, the result, the rake and payouts; the hand’s tags and your notes; and the meaning of any of your own abbreviations used in them. Not sent: the place, the session, the date and time, your photos, your bankroll or any other hand. Names are sent as typed, and there is no switch to hide them.
  • Building or fixing a hand from words (the hand, your words, and the names of your places with their other names); building a hand from a photo of a table (the photos you choose, your shorthand notes, the names of your places); reading a receipt (the receipt photo); importing data (the whole files you choose, your notes, the names of your places); describing a face or an item (the words you type); ranges, stats and the Doodle Book “Ask Claude” (what you type and a summary of the thing you ask about); estimating an exchange rate (two currency codes and a date).
  • How long: what is kept by Anthropic is up to Anthropic and your account with it. The answers are kept on your phone. A batch waiting for its answer is kept on the hand (its number), and is in your backups.
  • Ground: you asked (each tap). Your key stays in your phone’s secure store, is sent only to Anthropic, and is left out of backups unless you switch on “Include Hero’s Claude key”.

You can also use any AI app without a key: the app copies the same text to your clipboard, or hands it to the share sheet, and you paste it into the AI app of your choice. What that app does with it is covered by its own privacy policy.

6. Exchange rates

  • What: two currency codes and a date (or a month), and, as with any website, your phone’s internet address, to free exchange-rate services that the app asks directly: Frankfurter (api.frankfurter.dev) first, then, if that cannot answer, currency-api (cdn.jsdelivr.net, with a second address on currency-api.pages.dev). Nothing else about you is sent. The app also asks our own address (api.doodlepoker.com) for the list of services and the order to ask them in: that is a plain request with no key and nothing about you.
  • When: if you have sessions, expenses or bankrolls in more than one currency, when the app opens (today’s rates, once a day) and when you ask. You can turn to typing the rates yourself instead.
  • Why: to turn amounts from one currency into another.
  • How long: up to that service; we do not receive it.
  • Ground: you asked (it is how the app shows amounts in your home currency).

7. Calendar, browser and phone links

  • What and when: when you add a planned session, or the due date of an IOU, to your calendar, the app writes it to your phone’s calendar, or opens Google, Outlook or Yahoo Calendar in your browser, as you choose. The event contains a link to doodlepoker.com/open/, a page on this website that only passes you back to the app. An IOU’s event says who and what, never an amount. A reminder for an IOU is a notification the phone itself shows, and says who and what, never an amount. No server is involved in reminders.
  • When you tap a place’s queue number, your phone starts a call or opens its message app with a text ready. You send it.
  • Ground: you asked.

8. Backups, exports and sharing

Backups, exports (spreadsheets, text, PDF) and “share” (as text or as a picture) are made only when you ask. They are files or text handed to your phone’s share sheet or clipboard, and where they go is up to you. The app never sends them anywhere itself. A backup holds everything the app keeps, including your record of agreeing to this statement; keep it safe. A backup never holds the key of your encrypted copy.

9. An encrypted copy of your records (the cloud copy)

  • What: your records (sessions, hands, people, places, expenses, trips, bankrolls and your settings, the ones that are yours and not your phone’s), each encrypted on your phone, on its own, before it is sent, with a key that only your devices have. We cannot read it and nobody else can. All we can see is: a code made from your key (it cannot be turned back into the key), a reference for each record that does not say what kind of thing it is, when each record was last changed, whether it is deleted, and how big it is. The name you give each of your devices is sent encrypted with the rest. Pictures (the Doodle Book, photos, receipts) are not sent.
  • The key: a secret your phone makes (32 random bytes) and keeps in its secure store. It never leaves your devices unless you show it, copy it, add a device with a code (the code is good for ten minutes and for one use) or, only if you choose to add an email address, lock it with us (see 10); and it is never in a backup. From it come the number we know your copy by, a secret that proves a call is yours (we keep only an encrypted copy of that, as we do for the key in 2), and the key that encrypts the records. If you lose every device and the key, nobody, including us, can read the copy.
  • When: only if you choose “Yes, keep a copy” on the first screen, or switch it on later in Settings, Sync. It is then sent when the app opens, a little while after a change, and when the app goes to the background. “Not now” sends nothing at all. You can switch it off at any time.
  • Why: so your records are not lost with your phone, and so you can use them on another device.
  • Where: Microsoft Azure servers in the United States.
  • After a deletion: when you delete the copy (on a phone or in a linked browser) we keep a small marker for twelve months, holding only the code of the copy, the time, and whether a phone or a browser deleted it. It is so that your other devices find out and switch their cloud copy off instead of putting it back; they keep all their records. Turning the copy on again starts a fresh one and clears the marker. A copy removed by the sweep for silence leaves no marker.
  • How long: until you delete it (Settings, Sync, Delete Hero’s copy in the cloud, which deletes everything of it at once; or in a linked browser, on its page Delete Hero’s copy in the cloud), or until twelve months pass with no contact from any of your devices, when a daily sweep deletes all of it; your phone still has its own records and sends them again if you come back. A record you delete stays as a small marker (without its contents) for about 90 days, so the deletion reaches your other devices. The short-lived envelope for adding a device is deleted when it is used, or after ten minutes. We also count how many calls a minute a copy and an internet address make, and keep the counts for a day or two.
  • Ground: your agreement (you chose it), and our interest in keeping the service safe (the counts).

11. A browser linked to your phone (the website’s app, only if you link one)

  • What: to link a browser, the website shows a code; your phone scans it and leaves a small envelope in our service that only that browser can open. In it are what the browser needs to read your encrypted copy (the key that opens the records and the key of their references, never the key that lets a device change anything) and a pass. Our service keeps, for each linked browser, a name for it (such as “Chrome on Windows”), when it was linked, when it was last used and a one-way hash (a hash) of its pass, so it cannot give out a working pass. The browser keeps, in that browser only: the keys, locked by a key that the page can use but cannot read out; your records, still encrypted; and, if you tick “Remember this browser”, a small mark (a cookie for doodlepoker.com) that says only “linked”, never the key, the pass, a name or an id, so the home page can offer to open your poker. Without the tick, the browser forgets all of it when the tab is closed.
  • What it can do: read your encrypted copy; delete the whole copy (after you type the word DELETE in that browser), which also ends every linked browser’s pass; and, for a browser linked after this was added, take down what you have published (it is given the key that opens the sealed keys of your published hands and live pages). It cannot add, change or delete a record: the cloud refuses every such change made with a browser’s pass. Take a browser off on your phone and it can do none of it.
  • When: only if you link a browser: you scan its code on your phone and check the short code against its screen.
  • Why: so you can look at your sessions and hands on a bigger screen without an account.
  • Where: the pass and the envelope are kept on Microsoft Azure in the United States, and the app itself is served by GitHub Pages, at app.doodlepoker.com.
  • How long: until you take the browser off on your phone (Settings, Sync), or forget it in the browser, or it is not used for twelve months, when a daily sweep removes its pass; the envelope is deleted when the browser fetches it, or after ten minutes. A browser keeps its own copy until you forget it or clear its data.
  • Ground: your agreement (you linked it).

10. An email address, so your key can always be got back (only if you add one)

  • What: the email address you type, only while a one-time code is asked for and checked (it is not kept); a fingerprint of it (the address tidied and put through a keyed one-way hash, with a secret that lives in our key service, so that nobody who holds the store can find out who is in it); and your sync key, locked with a master key that lives in our key service. These are kept as one entry for each address, in a store of its own, apart from the one your encrypted records are in, with its own permissions. A pending code is kept only as a keyed one-way hash, for ten minutes. We also count the codes asked for from an address, a device and an internet address (the address is kept only as a hash), for a day or two.
  • What it means: with an email address your key is kept with us, locked and apart from your records, so we can give it back to you. Without one, only you have it. Anyone who can put code on our service, or who can sign in to our Azure account, can reach everything we keep, including a locked key and the means to unlock it; we protect that sign-in with two-step sign-in. Someone who broke into one store alone could read nothing.
  • When: only if you choose to add an address in Settings, Sync. To add it, to take it off, or to get your records back on a new phone you type the address and are sent a one-time code (six digits, good for ten minutes, a few tries only, with limits on how many are sent to one address and from one device). There is no password. On a new phone the key comes back sealed so that only that phone can open it.
  • Test mode: until our mail service is set up, the service is in test mode: no email is sent, one fixed code works for every address, and the app says TEST MODE. This is for building the app and is not for real use.
  • Why: so a player who loses every device and never kept the key can still get their records back.
  • Where: Microsoft Azure in the United States (a separate storage account, and Azure Key Vault for the master key and the fingerprint secret, whose log of every use is switched on). The mail service that sends the codes will be named in “Who else handles it” when it is set up.
  • How long: until you take the address off in Settings, Sync (which deletes the kept key at once; “Delete Hero’s copy in the cloud” offers it too, and does not do it by itself), or until you write to us to have it deleted. Codes are deleted when used or after ten minutes; the counts after about two days.
  • Ground: your agreement (you chose it).

What the app asks your phone for

Each of these is asked for only when needed, and used on your phone:

  • Camera and photos: when you take or pick a photo for a hand, a receipt or the Doodle Book, and when you scan the code of another device to add it to your encrypted copy, or the code of a browser to link it (the picture of the code is read on the phone and is not kept or sent).
  • Files: when you choose a backup to restore or files to import.
  • Calendar: to add a planned session, or the due date of an IOU, when you ask.
  • Notifications: reminders the phone itself shows (breaks, stack updates, missions, a session’s time limit, an IOU that is due).
  • Fingerprint, face or screen lock: only to unlock the app, if you switch the lock on. The app never sees your fingerprint or face.
  • Vibration, motion and battery level: for feel, for pictures that swing when you tilt the phone, and to slow the animation on a low battery. These stay on the phone.

The app does not use your location, contacts or microphone.

Where it is kept, and who else handles it

Where. What you publish, your live pages, the record of your agreement and, if you keep one, your encrypted copy (and, if you add an email address, your locked key, in a store of its own) are kept on Microsoft Azure servers in the United States. This website, and its app at app.doodlepoker.com, are hosted by GitHub Pages (also in the United States). So when you use these, information leaves your own country. We rely on the safeguards for such transfers that these companies offer under the laws above (for example standard contractual clauses), and we send nothing there that you have not chosen to send.

Who else handles it.

  • Microsoft (Azure: storage and the small service we run) and GitHub (this website), as our hosts: they may keep ordinary server logs under their own policies.
  • Anthropic, for the Claude features, only with your own key and under your own account (see 5).
  • The exchange-rate services (Frankfurter, and currency-api through jsDelivr or Cloudflare Pages), for the lookup in 6.
  • Google, Microsoft or Yahoo, only if you choose their calendar in your browser (7).
  • The mail service of the contact address (mail you send us is read in an ordinary email account).
  • A mail service for the one-time codes, if you add an email address (see 10): it sees your address and the code when one is sent. None is set up yet: until it is, the codes are not emailed (test mode).
  • Google or Apple, when you download the app, under their own policies; we receive no personal information from them.

We do not sell your information, we do not share it for advertising, and we do not use it to profile you.

Your rights

Wherever you are, you may ask us to show what we keep about you, correct it, delete it, give it to you in a form you can take away (portability), restrict it or object to its use, and you may withdraw your agreement at any time (withdrawing does not undo what was done before). The app lets you do most of this yourself: your records are on your phone, a backup is your copy, a published hand or live page can be taken down at once in Settings, Hero’s published hands and live pages, and your encrypted copy can be deleted at once in Settings, Sync. Because we cannot read the encrypted copy, we cannot show you what is in it: your records are on your devices. For anything we keep, such as the record of your agreement, write to us (see “Who is responsible”) with your reference, and we will deal with it within a month. We make no decisions about you by machine.

If you are in California, you also have the right to know, delete, correct and limit the use of your personal information, and not to be treated worse for using these rights. We do not sell or share personal information, and we do not use or disclose sensitive personal information except to give you the app.

If you are not happy with how we have dealt with your information, you may complain to the regulator of your own country. For example: in New Zealand, the Office of the Privacy Commissioner; in the United Kingdom, the Information Commissioner’s Office; in the European Union, the data protection authority of your country (the European Data Protection Board lists them); in Australia, the Office of the Australian Information Commissioner; in Canada, the Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada; in California, the California Privacy Protection Agency.

Age

Doodle Poker is for adults: 18 and over. It is not meant for children, and we do not knowingly keep anything about a child.

Your agreement

The first time you open the app, and again when this statement changes in a way that matters to you, the app asks you to tick that you have read and agree to it, and cannot be used until you do. The app keeps, on your phone, which version you agreed to, when, the app’s version and what you chose; the app never deletes it, it is in your backups, and About shows it. Once you publish a hand, turn on a live page or keep an encrypted copy, the same record is also kept in the cloud (see 4); turning the copy off, and deleting it, are added to it.

Keeping it safe

Everything sent from the app goes over an encrypted connection. What you publish is readable by anyone with its link, by design. The encrypted copy of your records is useless without your key, and a key kept for an email address is locked with a master key that lives in a separate key service. If what we keep were ever lost or exposed in a way that could harm you, we would say so on this website and tell the regulators as the laws require.

This website

This website uses no analytics and no scripts from anyone else, and no cookies except one: if you link a browser and tick “Remember this browser”, the app at app.doodlepoker.com leaves a small mark (a cookie for doodlepoker.com that says only “linked”, never a key or a name) so the home page can offer “Hero’s poker”; forgetting the browser removes it. and its fonts are served from the site itself. The share buttons at the foot of each page are ordinary links to Facebook, X, WhatsApp, Telegram, Reddit, LinkedIn and your email: nothing is loaded from those sites, and they learn of your visit only if you tap one. GitHub Pages, like any host, may keep ordinary server logs; we do not receive them.

Changes to this statement

This statement has a version number and a date. When it changes in a way that matters to you (a new thing kept or sent, a new place, a new company), the version goes up, the app shows its first screen again with what has changed, and asks you again. A change of wording only (a typo, a clearer sentence) keeps the version and does not ask again; the date it was last improved is shown. The website always shows the newest version at privacy.html. The app keeps which version you agreed to, and when. If you want to read an earlier version, write to privacy@doodlepoker.com and we will send you its address.
